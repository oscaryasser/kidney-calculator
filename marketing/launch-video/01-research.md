# What the best health apps and videos of 2021–2026 actually do

Research notes behind the launch video. Each item is here because it changed a decision in the brief, script, or prompts.

## Apps that set the bar

**Gentler Streak** (Apple Watch App of the Year 2022, Apple Design Award 2024, Social Impact). Its whole pitch is tone: no shame, no red streak-breaking, rest is counted as progress. Health data is organised around one question ("how are you doing today?") rather than a dashboard of everything. Our audience is 58 and has just been told a number is "a little low." Tone matters more than features.

**Oura**. Sells one number (Readiness) and ties it to identity outcomes: living longer, feeling like yourself. The ring is shown as an object you'd wear anyway. Lesson: pick one number and make it the hero. For us that number is eGFR, and the hero visual is the gauge on the home screen.

**WHOOP**. Also one number (Recovery). Its best-performing video ads split the promise into three verbs (Sleep Better, Feel Better, Train Better) and put the free-trial CTA on the first frame. Lesson: three verbs, not ten features. Ours are Check, Pick, Log.

**Flo**. Grew on TikTok through creators telling their own story in their own kitchen, not studio ads. Lesson: a real-looking person in a real-looking place beats a rendered phone floating in space.

**Levels, Zoe**. Both turned a lab value into a line you watch move after something you did (a meal, a walk). That feedback loop is the reason people keep opening the app. Our Lab History screen already has that line. The video should make the line the emotional payoff.

**Headspace, Calm**. Launch videos that worked were simple, warm, and quiet. Calm's brand voice is a comforting narrator and almost no music. Both explain "how to use it" in seconds. Lesson: slow VO, no hype, one action per shot.

## Videos that worked

- Apple "Dear Apple" (Apple Watch, real letters read aloud). Health messaging lands hardest when it sounds like a person, not a brand.
- Apple Watch Series 10 "Quit Quitting" (2024). Short verbs, one idea, no feature list.
- NKF animated CKD series (2022 onward). Each video is 1.5–2.5 minutes, in English and Spanish, and translates eGFR and uACR into plain words. NKF made them because most people with CKD have never heard those terms.
- AKF "Know Your Kidney Numbers" (2024–2025). Reduced the entire disease to two tests, one blood and one urine. Over 700 people completed the tool in its first three months.
- Higgsfield Marketing Studio ad examples (2026). The ones that convert open with a human face and a problem in the first two seconds, then show the product screen as the answer, then end on a single CTA.

## Facts we can stand on

- About 35.5 million US adults have CKD and as many as 9 in 10 don't know it (CDC, updated March 2026).
- CKD has no symptoms until late. The only way to know is a number: eGFR from a blood test, uACR from a urine test.
- KDIGO 2024: physical activity 150 min/week, sodium under 2 g/day, no tobacco, SGLT2 inhibitors where indicated, and blood pressure control slow progression. The app's stated BP target is under 130/80, which is the ACC/AHA target. KDIGO's own target is systolic under 120 when tolerated. The video does not quote a number; the screen does.

## What this means for the video

1. Open on a person and a feeling, not the app. Two seconds, tops.
2. One hero number (eGFR gauge), one hero line (Lab History trend).
3. Three verbs anybody can do today: check your pressure, pick the green food, log your labs.
4. Warm narrator, slow pace, almost no music. The audience is 45–70.
5. Show Spanish on screen, not just in a caption. Hispanic adults carry a higher CKD burden and the app already ships in Spanish.
6. Never promise reversal. Promise visibility and steadiness. "A line you can keep steady" is honest; "watch it go up" is a claim we can't make.
7. End on a single CTA and a one-line disclaimer.

## Model facts that shaped the prompts

- Seedance 2.5 (ByteDance, released July 31, 2026) generates up to 30 seconds in a single pass, with picture and sound together, up to 50 references (images, video, audio), and timestamp-level editing. It is live on Higgsfield.
- Prompt syntax that Seedance 2.5 responds to: time ranges on their own lines (`0-5s:`), references as `@Image1`, spoken lines in `{curly braces}`, sound effects in `<angle brackets>`.
- On-screen text is garbled most of the time. Keep text out of the generation and add it in the editor.
- Higgsfield layers Cinema Studio (camera body, lens, 70+ camera presets), Soul (locked characters across shots), and Higgsfield Assist (prompt copilot) on top of Seedance 2.5. References go in before the prompt; era, genre, lighting, and physics are set as controls rather than described.
