# Creative brief: My Kidney Team launch video

## The app (from the App Store screenshots)

- Name on the home screen: **My Kidney Team**. Developer: Oscar Yasser Pena Zapata. Category: Health & Fitness, 13+. Languages: English and Spanish. iPhone and iPad.
- Store line: "Track labs, blood pressure, and kidney-friendly foods." Footer: "Not medical advice."
- Screens we own: Home with eGFR gauge (53.5, Stage G3a, A1, "+3"), Lab History line chart with coloured eGFR bands, Food Guide with K/P/Na traffic lights (Apple green, Apricot red, Arugula yellow), Blood Pressure with "Meets CKD Target" and a 30-day trend, and the Spanish Food Guide (Guía de alimentos).
- Look: iOS-native, white cards on soft grey, teal accent (about #3FB8B8), amber for stage 3a, green/yellow/red for food. Rounded, calm, no gradients.

## Who we're talking to

The sample profile in the app is a 58-year-old woman. That's the lead. She has diabetes or high blood pressure, her doctor said her "kidney number is a little low," and she left the appointment with a printout and no plan. She isn't sick. She isn't on dialysis. She's in the window where small habits still change the outcome, and nobody has told her that in plain words.

Secondary: her adult daughter or son who manages the family's health from their phone, and bilingual Hispanic households where the grandmother reads Spanish and the grandchild reads English.

## The one idea

**Kidneys don't hurt. They send numbers.**

CKD has no symptoms until late. That's why 9 in 10 people who have it don't know. The app turns the numbers that arrive on a lab printout into something a person can read, act on, and watch. The video shows three things anybody can do this week and the line they add up to.

Working title: **Watch your line.**

## Three verbs (the whole product in six words)

1. **Check** your pressure. (Blood Pressure screen)
2. **Pick** the green ones. (Food Guide)
3. **Log** your labs. (Lab History)

Every shot maps to one verb and one screen. Nothing else gets screen time.

## Tone

Warm, slow, unhurried. A narrator who sounds like a good nurse, not a brand. Morning light, a real kitchen, a real grocery aisle. No stock-footage doctors in white coats, no floating phones, no hospital corridors, no fear.

## Medical guardrails (non-negotiable)

- Never say or imply "reverse," "cure," "heal," or "fix" kidney disease.
- Never promise eGFR will go up. The honest promise is visibility and steadiness: "a line you can keep steady."
- Don't quote a BP target in VO. The screen shows the app's target; the narrator doesn't.
- Don't name medications.
- End card carries "Not medical advice. Talk to your care team." in readable size.
- Stage language matches the app: "Stage 3a, mild to moderate reduction." Nothing scarier.
